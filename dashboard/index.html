About this dashboard

Purpose. This is descriptive research from the Journey Health Foundation Research Center at the University of Tennessee at Chattanooga. It is intended to help funders, agencies, health systems, and community organizations see where conditions in our region are hardest and how many people are affected.

What it is not. It does not recommend investments, evaluate specific programs, or make eligibility determinations. Every designation and every score shown here should be verified with the administering agency before it drives a decision.

Scope. Currently 11 counties across the Tennessee pilot region (176 census tracts). 61 indicators drawn from the American Community Survey (2020–2024) and CDC PLACES (2024). Data source and vintage are shown on every indicator.

How to read it. Rates alone can mislead in low-population tracts. Read the tract count and suppression notes on the KPI panel. Rankings are point estimates; the uncertainty display will land alongside the ACS margins of error in a follow-on release. Race, ethnicity, and language indicators are shown as context only and never enter a score or ranking.

Contact. Journey Health Foundation Research Center, University of Tennessee at Chattanooga. mohith-addepalli@utc.edu

Add or remove counties using the Counties filter in the left sidebar. Each selected county becomes a column. The Best column highlights the county with the most favorable value for that indicator (only for indicators with a defined direction). Numbers shown are the unweighted mean of tract values in each county. This can differ from a population-weighted average when tracts within a county have very different populations.
Indicator

Which counties changed the most for the selected indicator?

Bars show how much a county's average value changed between the two most recent 5-year windows of Census ACS data: 2015–2019 and 2020–2024. Colors: green = change in a helpful direction, red = change in a harmful direction. Whether a rise or a fall is "helpful" depends on the indicator (falling poverty rate = green; rising median income = green).

How much did each tract change?

One bar per bucket of tracts. Tract-level change is noisier than the county average because tract populations are small — the county average smooths that noise out.

All tracts ranked — selected indicator

Sorted high to low across visible communities. Row shading marks the 10% of tracts showing greatest need and the 10% showing least need for this indicator, using its known direction (for example, higher poverty rate = greater need; higher broadband access = less need). Indicators without a defined direction (median age, race and ethnicity, etc.) are shown unshaded. Descriptive research output — not investment, lending, or siting advice.
