About this dashboard

What it is. Descriptive research from JHFRC (Journey Health Foundation Research Center, UT Chattanooga) on where community conditions are hardest across the 11-county Tennessee pilot region (176 census tracts, 61 indicators from ACS 2020–2024 and CDC PLACES 2024).

How to read it. Overview, KPIs, and Tract Ranking show tract-level values from the source. Compare Counties shows the published county estimate where available; a small ‡ marks universe-weighted tract aggregations (only permitted for indicators validated against official ACS). Blank means neither is available. 5-year change re-projects the 2015–19 baseline onto 2020 tract geometry so tracts whose boundaries shifted are not silently miscompared.

What it isn't. Not investment, lending, or eligibility advice. Verify every designation with the administering agency. Race, ethnicity, and language indicators are context only and never enter a composite score. Rates can mislead in small tracts — check the tract count and suppression notes on the KPI panel.

Compare counties side by side

Add or remove counties using the Counties filter in the left sidebar. Each selected county becomes a column. The Best column highlights the county with the most favorable value for that indicator (only for indicators with a defined direction). Numbers shown are the published county estimate from the Community Profiles source (ACS 2020–2024 or CDC PLACES 2024) where available; where a published county value is not available AND we have validated the metric identity against an official ACS county estimate, we compute universe-weighted aggregation of tract estimates using each indicator's measure-specific denominator, requiring at least 80% of the county's relevant population to be represented (marked with ‡). We do not use unweighted means, and we do not aggregate for indicators whose metric identity has not been validated — a defensible universe is not permission to aggregate. Blank cells mean neither a published value nor a validated aggregation is available. Cell shading shows position vs. the Tennessee benchmark for that indicator: shaded one tone if the county is more than 2% above the state value, the other tone if more than 2% below, and white if the county is within ±2% of the state benchmark (at parity) or if no state benchmark exists for that indicator. Shading is directionally neutral — it does not label values as favorable or unfavorable.

Which counties changed the most for the selected indicator?

Bars show how much a county's average value changed between the two most recent 5-year windows of Census ACS data: 2015–2019 and 2020–2024. Colors: green = change in a helpful direction, red = change in a harmful direction. Whether a rise or a fall is "helpful" depends on the indicator (falling poverty rate = green; rising median income = green).

How much did each tract change?

One bar per bucket of tracts. Tract-level change is noisier than the county average because tract populations are small — the county average smooths that noise out.
